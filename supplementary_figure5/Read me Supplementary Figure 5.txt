Read me: Supplementary Figure 5 – Hill-Based Predictions for the Nav1.7 Mutations F1449V and A1632G

Description:
This folder contains the code and data required to reproduce Supplementary
Figure 5, which shows Hill-based predictions of gating and firing changes for
the Nav1.7 mutations F1449V (panels a–c) and A1632G (panels d–f), each compared
with its study-specific wild-type (WT) control.

The figure includes:
- Panels a, d: Steady-state sodium activation (m_inf) and inactivation (h_inf)
  for WT and mutant
- Panels b, e: Membrane-potential responses to an applied current density of
  40 µA/cm² from 50 to 150 ms
- Panels c, f: Firing frequency as a function of applied current density
  (f–I curves)

Main Script:
- Supplementary_Figure_5.m
  → Generates the full figure (panels a–f)

Data Files (in this folder):
- Input data:
  - FV_fI_curve_second_method.csv → precomputed f–I data for F1449V (panel c)
  - AG_fI_curve_second_method.csv → precomputed f–I data for A1632G (panel f)
  

- Saved by the script (created each time it runs):
  - Supp5_traces.mat → the simulated voltage traces of panels b and e

Gating descriptors and Hill parameters are given in Supplementary Table S8.

Papers:
- Located in the "papers" folder:
  - DibHajj2005_F1449V.pdf and Yang2016_A1632G.pdf, the sources of the
    mutation gating changes
  - references.txt → full reference and DOI of each paper

Computation Notes:
- Panels a and d are computed directly from the Hill parameters written in
  the script.
- Panels b and e are simulated inside the script (ode113, 200 ms, current step
  from 50 to 150 ms).
- Panels c and f load the precomputed f–I data from the two CSV files.
- Runtime is fast (a few seconds).

Output:
- The script generates Supplementary_Figure_5.pdf and Supplementary_Figure_5.png,
  the same as Supplementary Figure 5 in the manuscript.

Requirements:
- No additional toolboxes required

Alt Text:
Six panels of Hill-based predictions for the Nav1.7 mutations F1449V (panels
a–c) and A1632G (panels d–f), each compared with its study-specific wild-type
(WT) control; WT is navy and mutant is orange. Panels a and d show steady-state
sodium activation (m∞, solid lines) and inactivation (h∞, dashed lines) from
−130 to 40 mV. In both mutants, activation is shifted to more negative voltages
and inactivation to more positive voltages, with a larger inactivation shift
for A1632G. Panels b and e show membrane-potential responses to 40 µA/cm²
applied from 50 to 150 ms: both mutants fire repetitively throughout the step,
whereas their WT models produce a single spike. Panels c and f show firing frequency as a function of
applied current density. The WT models fire at about 10 Hz once they start
firing. The mutant models start firing at lower currents and reach higher
rates, up to about 210 Hz for F1449V and 220 Hz for A1632G, followed by a sharp
decline at about 100 and 85 µA/cm², respectively.
